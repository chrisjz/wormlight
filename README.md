# Wormlight

A living _C. elegans_ in the browser. The worm's full connectome runs on the GPU and drives a physically simulated body on an agar plate, and its neurons glow as they activate, in the style of calcium imaging.

**Live:** https://chrisjz.github.io/wormlight/ (needs a browser with WebGPU).

**Status:** milestone 5, lesions and the brain swap, is done. The CPU reference simulates the connectome, the layers outside it and the body, but crawling as checkpoint 1 asks for it does not yet emerge ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), 2026-09-26). Research track R, the effort to change that, calibrated the planned model and then a model of its own ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), 2026-09-27). On R's fit the worm crawled in bouts, a bend travelling from head to tail, within checkpoint 1's frequency and wavelength bands but at under a third of a real worm's speed, and in only 8 of 20 trials for 20 s. Its B-type motor neurons carried the crawl, and its reversals, which the A-types made at the head switch's flips, came from the simulation's time step: at finer steps it barely reversed ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). A convergence study traced this to two causes, the white-noise current and the oscillators' stiff dynamics. A second study found that, at R's fit, a coloured noise current keeps the results within chance across steps, so the model now runs with one, on the CPU and the GPU alike, and track R fitted its model again with it ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), 2026-09-28). The refit's result doesn't depend on the step, but it fails checkpoint 1: the worm moves forward most of the time, at about an eighth of a real worm's speed, in runs of about 10 s, moved by its head's slow rhythm while its motor circuits stay quiet. By rules set before the refit, the refit is what the app now runs, and track R's first round has ended ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). A review then found R's model reaching partial inside its bounds, where the calibration ranked it lower for making no reversal, so a second round changed the calibration. One of its probe searches found a crawler that grades partial, even on checkpoint 1's own trials, but only the full run's fit could be chosen, and that run settled where the first round's did. So the refit stays, and the probe's crawler is recorded as exploratory. A third round, track R's last, was paused: an investigation found that crawl resting on two currents, the head switch's and proprioception's, that have no reversal potential and drive neurons far past any voltage a real one reaches, and the refit's own motion coming from the same switch. It also found the model without a backward mode: driving AVA, the reversal command neuron, makes no reversal, and the worm's reversals are slips at the head switch's flips. A conductance form of the two currents is now built beside them, and a survey of it found two of sixteen searches reaching a partial crawl, slow, without reversals and paced by the head switch alone, so that last round ran on it, by rules set before it ran: two phases of search on the crawl alone, a speed target of 0.15 body lengths per second, and its fit chosen only if its result doesn't depend on the time step. Its four picks crawled, three of them graded partial by checkpoint 1's measures over 200 trials, but each one's frequency and wavelength shifted at half the time step, so none could be chosen: the refit stays, and track R has ended ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), 2026-09-29). In the browser: the worm on a 10 cm agar dish whose wall stops it, beside a food lawn whose butanone field is drawn in faint isolines, and which the worm's AWC-ON neuron smells, adapting as Levy & Bargmann's threshold does. "Add food" drops more lawns, up to eight, which can be dragged, picked up and put down, or removed; their odour spreads and fades as the field is stepped on the GPU, and the URL carries them with the worm's seed, so a link reproduces the dish. The worm smells food but won't slow on it or dwell there: those need neuromodulation, which the model leaves out (spec §5). Clicking the worm, or the "Touch front" and "Touch back" buttons, touches it, holding the touch receptors whose fields cover that point 10 mV above rest for half a second. The worm's whole simulation, brain and body, is stepped on the GPU, where it matches the CPU reference in Chrome and in Safari ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), 2026-09-27). Beside it, the connectome as a rotatable 3D graph, with any neuron's connections lit, and an inspector that gives every connection's sign with its source and fidelity level. The inspector ablates the selected neuron, cutting every connection it has as a laser ablation does, and restores it; a Brain control swaps the real wiring for the contrast brain, one of ten rewirings of its chemical synapses that keep every neuron's numbers of inputs and outputs, run on the real wiring's fitted values untuned. Either change takes effect as the worm runs, and the URL carries both ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), 2026-09-30). You can pause the worm, slow it down or run it up to 10× faster. Its neurons don't glow yet (milestone 6). A behavioural harness runs checkpoint 0 on the CPU reference, which the silenced network passes in full, showing no crawling, touch reflex or chemotaxis (though that says little about the wiring: silenced, the refit's head switch, all that moves it, stays shut by the bound on its threshold), and checkpoint 1, crawling, which R's refit fails at both the 2.5 ms step and half of it; checkpoints 2 to 6, which need crawling, stay not reached, since track R ended below partial ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). The build spec is [WORMLIGHT_SPEC.md](WORMLIGHT_SPEC.md).

## Run it

Needs Node 22.22.1 or later (or 23.6 or later), and a browser with WebGPU (recent Chrome or Edge, or Safari 26 or later).

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```
